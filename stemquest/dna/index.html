Meet DNA

A tiny instruction library inside living things.

Before we extract it, let’s zoom in from a strawberry to the DNA packed inside its cells.

Strawberry

A strawberry is made of many tiny cells.

Cell

Most DNA is stored in a cell’s nucleus.

DNA

DNA carries instructions that help living things grow, work, and reproduce.

What is DNA?

DNA stands for deoxyribonucleic acid. It is a long molecule that stores biological instructions, a little like a recipe book for cells.

Where is it found?

DNA is inside the cells of plants, animals, fungi, and many microorganisms. That means strawberries have DNA—and so do you.

Why study it?

DNA can help scientists understand health, inherited traits, relationships between living things, and how organisms change over time.

How scientists use DNA

Open each example to learn more.

+MedicineUnderstanding health and designing treatments

Scientists study DNA to learn how some conditions develop and why a treatment may work differently for different people.

⌕ForensicsComparing biological evidence

Specialists can compare DNA samples as one part of a careful investigation. DNA evidence must always be collected and interpreted responsibly.

⌁Genetics researchStudying traits, variation, and inheritance

Researchers compare DNA to study how traits are passed on, how organisms are related, and how living things adapt.

Experiment guide

Five moves from fruit to visible DNA.

Complete each checkpoint when your presenter tells you to. Add a quick note if your group notices something useful.

Pause and follow your presenter. Do not taste any materials. Keep the extraction solution and alcohol away from your eyes and mouth.

  1. 1of 5

    Mash

    Mash the strawberries.

    Seal the strawberries in the bag and mash until there are very few large pieces left.

    Why do we mash strawberries?To break apart the strawberry tissue and help open cells so their DNA can be released.
  2. 2of 5

    Release

    Add the extraction solution.

    Add the amount your presenter gives you, reseal the bag, and mix gently.

    What is the solution doing?Soap helps break cell and nucleus membranes. Salt helps the released DNA gather together.
  3. 3of 5

    Separate

    Filter the mixture.

    Pour carefully through the filter and collect the liquid below it.

    Why filter?The filter holds back seeds, pulp, and larger cell pieces while the liquid containing DNA passes through.
  4. 4of 5

    Reveal

    Add cold alcohol.

    Tilt the cup and slowly add cold alcohol so it forms a layer. Do not stir.

    Why cold alcohol?DNA does not dissolve well in cold alcohol. It leaves the liquid and clumps into material we can see.
  5. 5of 5

    Observe

    Look for DNA strands.

    Watch the alcohol layer and the boundary between the layers. Use the tool your presenter provides to lift the material gently.

    What might DNA look like?Many DNA molecules clumped together can look white, cloudy, stringy, or web-like.
